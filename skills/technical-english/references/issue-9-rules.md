# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 rule inventory

This inventory paraphrases Part 1 of ASD-STE100 Issue 9. Use the official standard when this inventory and the source differ.

The General Recommendations are not STE rules. Apply them to prevent common writing errors.

## Section 1: Words

### Rule 1.1

Use only these word types:

- A word that the dictionary approves
- A technical noun
- A technical verb.

Use company glossaries and approved terminology sources when they are available.

### Rule 1.2

Use an approved word only as its specified part of speech.

A word can have more than one approved part of speech. Make its function clear from its position and context.

If a replacement changes the part of speech, change the sentence construction. Do not change the meaning.

### Rule 1.3

Use an approved word only with its approved meaning. Do not apply its unrestricted standard-English meanings.

### Rule 1.4

Use only the verb and adjective forms that the dictionary permits.

Use listed irregular forms. Use listed comparative and superlative forms where applicable.

### Rule 1.5

Use an eligible field term as a technical noun. The eligible categories include:

1. Official parts
2. Vehicles, machines, and their locations
3. Tools, support equipment, their parts, and their locations
4. Materials, consumables, contaminants, and unwanted material
5. Facilities, infrastructure, and logistics
6. Systems, components, circuits, functions, configurations, and parts
7. Mathematical, scientific, engineering, and formula terms
8. Navigation and geographic terms
9. Numbers, measurements, time, and symbols
10. Quoted labels, signs, markings, and display text
11. Roles, persons, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities
12. Body parts
13. Personal effects, food, and beverages
14. Medical terms
15. Official documents, document structures, standards, and guidelines
16. Environmental and operational conditions
17. Colors
18. Damage terms
19. Computer, information, and communication technology
20. Civil and military operations
21. Law and regulation
22. Animals, plants, and other life forms.

These categories alone do not approve a term. Use the term only in its applicable technical context.

Do not change official capitalization. Do not use comparative or superlative forms of color terms.

### Rule 1.6

Use an unapproved word only when it is all or part of an applicable technical noun.

This permission is specific to its technical context. Do not change an official term when the change gives a different term.

### Rule 1.7

Do not use a technical noun as a verb.

The same spelling can have both functions only when it independently satisfies Rules 1.5 and 1.12.

### Rule 1.8

Use the technical noun that the applicable company, industry, or subject field approves.

Use official parts data, project glossaries, and approved documentation as terminology sources.

### Rule 1.9

Select a new technical noun only when an approved term does not exist.

Select a short, clear term of no more than three words. Keep only the words that are necessary for identification.

### Rule 1.10

Do not use regional words, slang, or jargon as technical nouns.

### Rule 1.11

Use one technical noun for one item. Do not use synonyms or informal short forms for the same item.

Rule 2.2 gives the controlled method for a long official term.

### Rule 1.12

Use an eligible field action as a technical verb. Rule 1.12 has these categories and subcategories:

1. Manufacturing processes:
   - 1a. Remove material
   - 1b. Add material
   - 1c. Attach material
   - 1d. Change material strength, structure, or physical properties
   - 1e. Change the surface finish of material
   - 1f. Change the shape of material.
2. Computer processes and applications:
   - 2a. Input and output processes
   - 2b. User-interface and application processes
   - 2c. System operations.
3. Instructions and information for applicable subject fields:
   - 3a. Engineering, mathematical, and scientific contexts
   - 3b. Medical contexts
   - 3c. Civil and military operations
   - 3d. Navigation contexts
   - 3e. Automotive and railway contexts
   - 3f. Energy, oil, and gas contexts.
4. Law and regulation actions in legal and regulatory text.

The categories alone do not approve a verb. Use the verb only in its applicable context.

If an approved dictionary verb gives the correct meaning, use that approved verb.

Do not use a technical verb when approved words can give the same sentence.

Do not use a general or unclear technical verb. Use an approved verb with a technical noun when possible.

### Rule 1.13

Do not use a technical verb as a noun.

A permitted past participle can modify a technical noun. The same spelling can be a noun when it independently satisfies Rule 1.5.

### Rule 1.14

Use American English spelling.

Use different spelling when an applicable official directive requires it. Do not change spelling in immutable quoted text.

## Section 2: Multi-word nouns

### Rule 2.1

Use no more than three words in a multi-word noun.

Split a longer noun chain with prepositions, relative clauses, or direct verbs.

### Rule 2.2

Do not divide an official technical noun only because it has more than three words.

Write the full term at its first occurrence. If possible, then use an explained short form or an approved abbreviation.

Do not make an unnecessary abbreviation for a term of three words or fewer. Too many abbreviations make text difficult to read.

Use a hyphen only when words make one direct unit. Do not hyphenate a full noun chain to evade Rule 2.1.

Do not change an official hyphen. If the structure is uncertain, use an explanation, an approved short form, or an official abbreviation.

## Section 3: Verbs

### Rule 3.1

Use only the verb forms that the dictionary gives.

### Rule 3.2

Use only these forms and tenses:

- The infinitive
- The imperative
- The simple present
- The simple past
- The simple future
- The past participle as an adjective.

Do not use perfect, progressive, or other complex verb constructions.

### Rule 3.3

Use a listed past participle as an adjective only.

Put it before a noun or after `be`, `become`, or `stay`. Do not make an adjective from an unlisted participle.

### Rule 3.4

Do not use auxiliary verbs to make perfect, progressive, or complex passive constructions.

Use an approved simple tense, an imperative, or an active construction.

### Rule 3.5

Use an `-ing` verb form only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.

Do not use it as a progressive verb or as an unrestricted adjective.

The dictionary approves these fixed `-ing` words:

- Nouns: `lighting`, `opening`, `routing`, and `servicing`
- Adjectives: `mating`, `missing`, and `remaining`
- Pronoun: `something`
- Preposition: `during`.

### Rule 3.6

Use active voice.

In descriptive text, use passive voice only when the agent is unknown. Do not use this exception in procedural text.

Do not invent an incorrect agent. Make the known agent the subject, or use an applicable active construction.

Use the imperative in a procedure. Use `you` for the reader when necessary.

### Rule 3.7

Use an approved verb to describe an action. Do not replace the action with an abstract noun construction.

If an approved action verb does not exist, change the sentence construction.

## Section 4: Sentences

### Rule 4.1

Write short, clear, accurate sentences.

Give one instruction in a procedural sentence. Give one subject in a descriptive sentence.

Use concrete information. Remove ambiguity, and give applicable values when they improve accuracy.

### Rule 4.2

Do not omit words to make a sentence shorter. Do not use contractions.

Include the necessary subject, noun, verb, article, and antecedent. Write negative forms in full.

### Rule 4.3

Use a vertical list for complex text or a large set of items or actions.

Apply these controls:

- End the lead-in sentence with a colon.
- Identify each item with an applicable marker.
- Start each item with an uppercase letter.
- Include necessary articles.
- End each full sentence with a period.
- Do not end a fragment with a period, except for the final list item.
- Do not end an item with a comma or a semicolon.
- Connect each item grammatically and logically to the lead-in.
- Keep the items at one structural level.
- Do not mix procedural and descriptive items.
- Repeat `DO NOT` for each applicable safety item.

Follow the applicable publication rules for marker style.

### Rule 4.4

Connect related sentences with approved connecting words or phrases.

Use the connection to show sequence, contrast, simultaneous action, or result. A demonstrative adjective and noun can also make the connection.

In a procedure, connect an explanation to its work step. Connect safety information when necessary.

### Rule 4.5

Use an article or demonstrative adjective when grammar requires it.

Do not add an article to a general plural, mass noun, or abstract concept.

Use article placement to make modifier scope clear in a series. Do not put `the` before an immediately identifying alphanumeric identifier.

Use a comprehensive grammar reference when article use is uncertain.

## Section 5: Procedural writing

### Rule 5.1

Use no more than 20 words in each procedural sentence. This limit includes safety instructions.

A note sentence can have 25 words.

### Rule 5.2

Give one instruction in each sentence.

You can give two or more instructions together only when the actions occur at the same time.

Show the sequence of work steps clearly. A work step can include an action and its immediate result or limit.

### Rule 5.3

Write instructions in the imperative form.

Do not usually put `must` before an imperative. Use `must` only for an important safety instruction or condition when necessary.

### Rule 5.4

If the reader must know a condition before an instruction, put the condition first.

Put a comma between the prerequisite condition and the command. Do not apply this rule to all conditional clauses.

### Rule 5.5

Use a note only to give information. Apply the descriptive-writing rules to notes.

Do not put an instruction, requirement, limit, tolerance, required result, or safety instruction in a note.

Put a limit, tolerance, or result directly after its related action. Move essential information into the applicable work step.

Do a test of the procedure without its notes. The procedure must remain full and usable.

Use notes in descriptive text only when they are necessary for an illustration or table.

## Section 6: Descriptive writing

Descriptive text gives information. It does not give instructions and does not use the imperative.

### Rule 6.1

Give information gradually. Give one subject in each sentence.

### Rule 6.2

Use repeated key words and phrases to give the text a logical structure.

Use connecting words to show new, different, simultaneous, or resulting information. Do not change the selected key terminology.

### Rule 6.3

Use no more than 25 words in each descriptive sentence.

### Rule 6.4

Group related information in paragraphs.

Start each paragraph with a topic sentence. Use the other sentences to explain or add information about that topic.

### Rule 6.5

Give one topic in each paragraph.

Connect its topic sentence to earlier information. The topic sentences must make a useful outline when read separately.

### Rule 6.6

Use no more than six sentences in each paragraph.

Divide a longer paragraph. Do not combine different topics to avoid a new paragraph.

## Section 7: Safety instructions

A warning identifies possible injury or death. A caution identifies possible damage to objects.

### Rule 7.1

Identify the risk level with an applicable word or symbol.

Do a risk analysis before you select the level. Use a warning when injury or death is possible.

Use a caution for damage to machines, tools, equipment, or other objects. Use a warning when both risk levels apply.

Other domains can use different labels or symbols. Their content must still obey Rules 7.1 through 7.3.

STE does not require uppercase safety text. Use the applicable publication format.

### Rule 7.2

Start a safety instruction with a clear, accurate command or required condition.

Put a prerequisite condition before its action.

### Rule 7.3

Explain the specified risk or possible result when this information is available.

## Section 8: Punctuation and word count

STE does not define general punctuation style. Use an applicable authoritative style guide for other punctuation decisions.

### Rule 8.1

Use standard English punctuation. Do not use a semicolon. Write separate sentences instead.

### Rule 8.2

Use hyphens to connect words that are directly related.

Applicable groups include multi-word modifiers, compound numbers, fractions, shape terms, compound verbs, and some prefixed words.

A dash is not a hyphen. Do not use a dash as a substitute for a hyphen.

### Rule 8.3

Use parentheses only for these purposes:

- A reference
- An illustration or text identifier
- A work-step identifier
- An abbreviation
- Singular and plural forms together
- An explanation
- An alternative.

### Rule 8.4

In a vertical list, treat the lead-in colon as the end of a sentence for word count.

Use the applicable limit before the colon. Treat each item after the colon as a new sentence with its applicable limit.

### Rule 8.5

Count parenthetical text as one word in its containing sentence.

Also treat the text inside the parentheses as a separate sentence. Apply its sentence limit separately.

Count a parenthetical identifier or abbreviation as one word.

### Rule 8.6

Count each of these elements as one word:

- A number, including a written compound number
- A number with its unit
- A time abbreviation with its related number
- An abbreviation, acronym, or initialism
- An alphanumeric identifier
- Quoted text
- A formula treated as quoted text
- A title, heading, placard, or label
- A proper name of a person, group, organization, or geopolitical entity.

Do not count a number that only identifies a paragraph or work step.

Quoted text can use quotation marks, uppercase styling, or a different font.

### Rule 8.7

Count a hyphenated word or applicable hyphenated technical-noun group as one word.

## Section 9: Writing practices

### Rule 9.1

Change the sentence construction when a direct word replacement is not sufficient.

A direct replacement must keep the meaning, grammar, and the correct part of speech.

Change the construction when:

- The grammar must change.
- The direct replacement has no clear meaning.
- The suggested alternative changes the intended meaning.
- The word needs replacement but is not in the dictionary.

Determine the intended meaning and action first. Do not change the communication purpose.

You can divide sentences, remove unnecessary content, or request missing technical information.

After a change, make sure that nearby text remains correct. Immediate reader understanding is the primary objective.

### Rule 9.2

Use each approved word with its approved meaning and part of speech.

Read the applicable dictionary entry before use. Use context and position to clarify words with more than one approved function.

### Rule 9.3

Do not combine approved words to make an unapproved phrasal verb.

The dictionary approves a small number of phrasal verbs with restricted meanings. Examine the applicable entry.

### Rule 9.4

Use consistent terminology, wording, and sentence patterns.

Use the same item name and work-step wording when the context and meaning are the same.

Use consistent key terms and a logical structure in descriptive text.

## General Recommendations

### GR-1: The conjunction `that`

Use `that` after applicable verbs as much as possible. This use prevents ambiguity and helps translation.

### GR-2: The preposition `with`

Review each use of `with` for ambiguity.

Its approved meanings include association, shared help, and an instrument or means. Rewrite an ambiguous condition with the condition first.

Keep the primary action verb. Do not replace it with a general `use` command.

### GR-3: Pronouns

Use only dictionary-approved pronouns. Do not use `he` or `she`.

If a pronoun can refer to more than one noun, replace it with the applicable noun.

### GR-4: The pronoun `this`

Make the referent of `this` clear. If more than one referent is possible, give the applicable context again.

### GR-5: False friends

Make sure that the word has its English meaning, not a different meaning from the writer's first language.

### GR-6: Latin abbreviations

Use English words instead of Latin abbreviations. Replace `e.g.` with `for example` and `i.e.` with `that is`.

Replace `etc.` with the necessary information, or remove it when it adds no information.

### GR-7: Inclusive language

Use neutral, non-discriminatory, and gender-neutral language.

Do not use `he`, `she`, `man`, or `woman`. Use `man` or `woman` only when the applicable context requires it.

Follow applicable international, national, company, or industry guidance.

### GR-8: Possessive form

Use the apostrophe possessive only when the construction is correct.

If you are not sure, use a different construction. Follow applicable publication specifications and style guides.
